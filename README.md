## The pick

The judge weighs, in order: the joke is about office life and lands as a joke, the observation is true to the office, the turn is surprising rather than a pun or a stock line, it is tight, and it is original. This line is built for exactly that order:

- **True first.** The meeting about meetings is itself a meeting, and it behaves like one: it runs over because everyone has thoughts. The nod comes before the laugh.
- **The turn.** No pun, no stock "could have been an email" line — the joke escalates. The cure for meetings reproduces the disease one level down, and the punchline is the last four words: *a pre-meeting for the follow-up*.
- **Tight.** Three clauses in one breath — setup, cause, consequence — and every word earns its place when read aloud.
- **Fresh.** The angle is the self-defeating fix, not the meeting itself.

Runners-up considered: the printer that has warned about low toner for three years (the only employee whose alerts everyone reads, believes and ignores), and the coffee machine's four minutes as the day's only stand-up where something gets delivered. The meeting line won on truth: it needs no prop and no wordplay.
